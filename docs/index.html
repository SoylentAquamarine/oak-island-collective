Goal #1 · Find out what is actually documented

We are trying to find out if there is anything genuine to decipher.
We don't yet know if there is.

The Money Pit's 90-foot stone — the object said to carry a cipher — has been lost since roughly the 1860s. Our first goal is not to crack a code; it's to determine, from the documented record, whether a genuine code ever existed at all. Our second goal is to document every step here. Our third is to publish the useful things we discover along the way — including mistakes, dead ends, and an honest "nothing to decipher" if that's where the evidence leads. Read on for what "started" actually means.

Why this project might legitimately find nothing

The stone itself no longer exists.

Our sibling investigations of the Voynich manuscript and rongorongo are both built around a genuine, physically surviving, undeciphered corpus — the only open question is what it says. Oak Island's 90-foot stone is lost, known only through disputed secondhand transcriptions, and the popular "solved cipher" story's own history is itself disputed — some researchers trace it only to 20th-century popular literature. This project may conclude, honestly and completely, that there is nothing verifiable left to decipher. That would be a real, valuable finding, not a failure.

01

Historian

Traces every artifact and claim to its earliest verifiable documented source, trust-tiered by era.

02

03

Tests the claimed cipher, only against the transcription provenance work finds most credible.

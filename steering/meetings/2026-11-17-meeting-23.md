## Evidence reviewed
I directly read the Archive.org OCR for *Collier's: The National Weekly*, Vol. 47, Issue 22, dated 19 August 1911 (item `sim_colliers-the-national-weekly_1911-08-19_47_22`). In his first-person article “Solving the Mystery of Oak Island,” H. L. Bowdoin says he examined the reported 90-foot stone at Creighton's bookbindery in Halifax during his 1909 expedition and describes the rock as “basalt,” “hard and fine-grained.” The same passage reports that the alleged characters were “supposed to mean” “Ten feet below two million pounds lie buried.” This is a primary publication and a first-person account of the 1909 examination, but this cycle read the OCR text only; the page image was not visually rechecked. The Archive.org issue metadata and OCR identify the publication date as 19 August 1911, correcting secondary chronologies that give 18 August.

The existing record separately identifies direct primary scans of the 13 and 20 August 1857 *Liverpool Transcript* letters, a negative 6 August issue check, and the 2 January 1864 *Colonist* text as a named 2014 compilation transcription rather than the original scan.

## Falsification and uncertainty
Bowdoin's statement documents what he says he saw and what he had been told; it does not authenticate an inscription or establish a reading. The 1864 transcription is not the original scan and describes events that predate its publication. The missing stone prevents new physical inspection. If no independently sourced inscription image, rubbing, or reliable transcription survives, the defensible endpoint may be a provenance history rather than cryptanalysis.

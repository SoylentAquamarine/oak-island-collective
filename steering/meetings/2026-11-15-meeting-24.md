## Goal and evidence ladder
Establish whether a genuine inscription is documented by contemporary evidence before any cryptanalysis. A defensible conclusion that no verifiable cipher survives is acceptable.

## Evidence and uncertainty
- The complete 6 August 1857 Liverpool Transcript issue (24 columns) contains no Oak Island/Kidd/treasure mention. This rules out that issue only.
- The 2 January 1864 The Colonist chapter is available as a 2014 compilation/transcription, not a verified original scan. It reports a flagstone with cut letters and figures, engraved side down, and Smith-family custody.
- The phrase “did not appear to be in their own vernacular” is the 1864 chapter author's reported characterization of earlier events; the record does not establish that it is a verbatim statement by an eyewitness or that the author personally saw the stone.
- The 27 January 1864 Cooke/Hunter-Duvar correspondence is a separate, still-unlocated archival lead. The prepared Nova Scotia Archives inquiry remains unsent.

## Falsification and ethics
Do not infer a cipher from a later transcription or turn one negative issue search into a general absence claim. Preserve publication date, event date, author, and source tier separately. Respect archival reproduction terms; do not contact institutions on the user's behalf without authorization.

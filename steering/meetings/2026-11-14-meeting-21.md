## Evidence reviewed
- The 6 August 1857 Liverpool Transcript issue was read across all 24 columns and contains no Oak Island/Kidd/treasure reference. This eliminates that issue only.
- A 2014 compilation reproduces a 2 January 1864 *The Colonist* chapter, “History of the Oak Island Enterprise,” describing a flagstone about two feet by one foot with rudely cut letters and figures, its engraved side down, and reported Smith-family custody. This is the richest stone description found in the project record so far.
- The 1864 text is a compiled transcription, not the original newspaper scan. It narrates earlier events, so its publication date does not prove the writer personally saw the stone. The separate 27 January 1864 George Cooke reply / Hunter-Duvar correspondence is a different, still-unlocated archival lead; the existing inquiry is drafted but unsent.

## Evidence and falsification rules
A negative search of one issue or archive holding cannot prove the stone never existed. A secondhand transcription cannot be promoted by a plausible decoding. Preserve the distinction between the 2 January Colonist chapter and the separate Cooke correspondence. A valid endpoint may be that no genuine cipher can be verified.

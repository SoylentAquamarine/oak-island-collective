# Steering Committee Meeting #54 — 2026-11-22 14:21 ET

**Project goal.** Establish what is documented about the alleged 90-foot stone and later cipher story. Cryptanalysis remains conditional on authenticating a primary inscription or defensible contemporary transcription.

## Evidence reviewed
- Read the Internet Archive OCR for *Collier's: The National Weekly*, 19 August 1911, Vol. 47, Issue 22, article “Solving the Mystery of Oak Island,” printed pages 19–20: https://archive.org/details/sim_colliers-the-national-weekly_1911-08-19_47_22
- Bowdoin's first-person account says his party examined the stone at a Halifax bookbindery in 1909; he describes it as hard, fine-grained basalt and reports that the supposed characters were said to mean “Ten feet below two million pounds lie buried.” His sixth conclusion denies that a system of characters existed. The OCR is visibly garbled in part of that sixth-conclusion passage.
- This is a primary source for Bowdoin's 1909 observations and his 1911 argument, not proof that an earlier inscription never existed. The issue's OCR was read; page images were not visually inspected.
- The official reel-2634 catalogue range covers the alleged 23 February 1912 Blair reply date, but the exact issue and reply remain unread.

## Evidence standard and falsification
Require an original issue/page, exact date/title/page/column, full context and a reproducible transcription. If the 1912 issue is absent or the alleged reply is not present, retire that lead. Treat Bowdoin's testimony as evidence of his account, not as a substitute for the missing stone or an independent proof of its origin.

## Wasted effort, blockers and capacity
Broad web searching has diminishing returns now that the 1911 primary issue is located. OCR is useful for discovery but must be checked against page images before quoting disputed wording. The next bottleneck is the 1912 issue, not cryptanalysis or compute.

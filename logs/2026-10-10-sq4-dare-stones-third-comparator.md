# SQ-4 — third comparator case: the Dare Stones

**Trigger:** SQ-4's own status trail named "a third case in a future cycle" as the natural next step once
the Beale and Kensington panel was no longer thin. With this cycle's other active threads either genuinely
blocked (archive inquiry, Georgiev) or awaiting a collaborative decision (SQ-3 sequencing), this is real,
unblocked parallel work exactly matching this sidequest's own standing scope ("SQ-4 can begin in parallel
with SQ-1/SQ-2... without competing with the primary task").

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known about Oak Island's own record: the Beale and Kensington panels both found matches clustering
around provenance/promotion/monetization, with anything requiring the lost original stone's own content
staying blocked by SQ-2's own transcription problem. Not done: a third, independently-built comparator with
a different structural profile — Beale and Kensington are both "claimed decoded message" or "claimed
inscription" cases with a single central promoter; Dare Stones is structurally different — a 42-48-stone
*series*, supplied by a paid forger over time, exposed via a documented extortion attempt.

## Design and why it's non-circular

Directly fetched (not search-summary) NCpedia's "Dare Stones" article — built entirely from
Dare-Stones-specific history (Boyden Sparkes's 1941 *Saturday Evening Post* investigation, 2016 UNC
Asheville materials testing), none of it aware of or tuned to Oak Island. Checking Oak Island's
already-on-file record against this independently-built set is a real generalization test, not a
third pass confirming the same conclusion differently.

## The Dare Stones: documented, named fabrication signatures

1. **A paid supplier with a documented prior history of similar forgery**: Georgia stonemason Bill
   Eberhardt supplied 42 of the 48 total stones (all later judged forgeries) after a $500 reward was
   offered for a second stone — investigative reporter Boyden Sparkes found Eberhardt "had previously sold
   forged Indian relics," a specific, named, pre-existing fabrication history, not a one-off incident.
2. **Linguistic anachronism and unnaturally consistent spelling**: Sparkes's 1941 exposé specifically
   cited "anachronistic language" and — a subtler, less obvious tell — "unusually consistent spelling,"
   since genuine 16th-century English had no standardized orthography; real period writing should show
   *more* spelling variation, not less.
3. **Names absent from the independently-documented historical record**: colonist names appearing on the
   stones that don't appear in any other surviving record of the Roanoke colony.
4. **A direct, self-incriminating confession-level artifact**: when Pearce stopped paying, Eberhardt
   produced a stone reading "Pearce and Dare Historical Hoaxes. We Dare Anything" and tried to extort $200
   for it — unambiguous, first-person evidence of fabrication, not an inference.
5. **A genuinely unresolved partial exception within an otherwise-debunked series**: the first stone found
   (the Chowan River Stone, 1937) remains disputed even today — a 2016 UNC Asheville materials test found a
   weathering contrast between the stone's interior and its engravings that some read as a sign of real age,
   while linguists dispute this on word-usage and signature-convention grounds. Scholarly consensus treats
   the other 47 stones as forgeries but does not close the case on this one.

## Applied comparison against Oak Island's own record

| Dare Stones signature | Oak Island equivalent, already on file |
|---|---|
| A paid supplier with documented prior forgery history | **Not yet checkable — a real gap, not a miss**: Oak Island's attribution chain (Kempton 1949, the anonymous 1894/1949-era tellers) has never been checked against any of these individuals' own prior history of fabrication elsewhere. Unlike Dare Stones, no investigative-journalism-style background check (a Sparkes-equivalent) exists in the record this project has assembled. A concrete, specific lead for a future cycle. |
| Linguistic anachronism / unnaturally consistent spelling | **Partially blocked, same as the other two cases**: vocabulary-dating remains foreclosed by the original stone's own loss (same disanalogy already logged for Kensington). The "unnaturally consistent spelling" angle specifically has never been tried against Kempton's own 1949 cipher key text — a new, concrete, checkable idea this comparator contributes that neither Beale nor Kensington suggested. |
| Names absent from the independently-documented record | **Already effectively tested, a real match**: this is structurally the same test already run via the 1949 attribution chain's own verifiable misidentification (the real professor was Swiss, later misremembered/reported as Irish) — a documented instance of the chain's own details not holding up against independent verification, the same signature this comparator names directly. |
| A direct, self-incriminating confession-level artifact | **No equivalent found — an informative disanalogy, not a miss**: nothing in Oak Island's record resembles Eberhardt's extortion stone. This is a meaningful asymmetry worth naming: Dare Stones collapsed because a named, living, identifiable forger turned on his own patron; Oak Island's story, whatever its merits, has no equivalent single point of first-person collapse — its evidentiary problems are all structural/provenance-based (undocumented chains, late attribution, self-inconsistent keys), not a confession. |
| A genuinely unresolved partial exception within a debunked series | **A real, useful parallel for calibrating this project's own epistemic posture**: Dare Stones shows that even a thoroughly-debunked hoax series can contain one piece that responsible scholarship still won't fully close out, decades later. Oak Island's own SQ-2 finding (no reliable ground truth transcription exists, not because everything is proven fake but because nothing can be checked at all) is a different *kind* of unresolved case — this project's own unresolved status is an evidentiary vacuum, not a contested test result like the Chowan River Stone's. Worth keeping this distinction explicit rather than treating "unresolved" as one undifferentiated category. |

## Decision

A third, independently-sourced comparator with a genuinely different structural profile (a forger-supplied
series exposed by extortion, not a single promoted artifact) still converges on the same overall pattern
this project's panel has found twice already — provenance and attribution-chain problems are the real,
checkable signal, not inscription content (which stays blocked across all three comparators by Oak Island's
own lost-original problem). Two new, concrete, not-yet-attempted checks surfaced directly from this
comparator: (1) a background check on Kempton's and the 1949-era tellers' own prior history, matching the
Sparkes-style investigative approach; (2) a spelling-consistency check on Kempton's own 1949 cipher key
text, the same "too consistent to be period-authentic" signature Sparkes used. SQ-4's panel is now
three cases deep and, per this sidequest's own earlier call, substantively past the point of thinness —
future cycles should weigh pursuing one of these two new concrete leads over a fourth comparator case.

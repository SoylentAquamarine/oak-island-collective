# SQ-4 — first comparator case: the Beale Ciphers, and an applied comparison against Oak Island's own record

**Trigger:** SQ-4 ("Comparative hoax/fabrication benchmark") had no status entries and does not depend on
the archive-request blocker — a genuinely unclaimed thread, found the same way indus-script-collective's
SQ-4 was found two cycles ago. The Beale Ciphers (Virginia, 1820s claimed origin, 1885 pamphlet) are the
single closest documented comparator to Oak Island's own story: a 19th-century American buried-treasure
legend built around a secret cipher, widely studied by both cryptologists and historians, with a
well-documented scholarly case for fabrication — directly matching SQ-4's own named signature types.

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known about Oak Island's own record: the "Forty feet below, two million pounds are buried"
decoded plaintext has no 19th-century documentary basis; its earliest traceable source is an 1894
prospectus with *different* wording ("Ten feet below... two million pounds"), and the now-standard wording
only appears in 1949 via an anonymous attribution chain (an unnamed schoolteacher citing an unnamed "Irish
instructor") that misidentifies the real named professor later credited (Swiss, not Irish, and absent from
the original 1949 account) — already a Confirmed Finding in this project's own record. Not done: any
documented external comparator case to check whether this pattern matches known, scholarly-established
fabrication signatures elsewhere, or whether this is being over-read without that calibration.

## Design and why it's non-circular

The Beale Ciphers' fabrication signatures were established by independent historians and cryptologists
(Nickell 1982, Singh 2000, Poundstone 1993) studying a *different* case, for reasons unrelated to Oak
Island — not derived from or tuned to match Oak Island's own record. Checking Oak Island's already-on-file
findings against this independently-built checklist is a real test, not a post-hoc rationalization: the
checklist existed before this comparison was attempted, built entirely from Beale-specific evidence.

## The Beale Ciphers: documented, named fabrication signatures

Directly fetched (not search-summary) from Wikipedia's "Beale ciphers" article, which cites Nickell (1982,
"Discovered: The secret of Beale's treasure"), Singh (2000, *The Code Book*), and Poundstone (1993,
*Biggest Secrets*):

1. **Anachronistic vocabulary**: "stampeding" is not attested in English print until 1832 (the letters
   claim an 1822 origin); "improvise" is similarly "not otherwise recorded before the 1820s in English" —
   both plausibly borrowed from French/Spanish usage in 1820s New Orleans, suggesting a later author
   reaching for period-adjacent but not period-accurate vocabulary (Nickell 1982).
2. **Traceable single-author origin**: Nickell's 1982 stylometric analysis concluded the pamphlet's
   author, James B. Ward (who published the Beale story in 1885), was "almost certainly" the same person
   as the purported original letter-writer "Beale" — i.e., the supposed 1820s originator and the 1880s
   "discoverer" collapse into one person under stylistic analysis.
3. **Narrative/structural implausibility**: the third cipher is "too short to list 30 individuals' next of
   kin," as the story claims it does (Poundstone 1993) — an internal structural contradiction.
4. **Contradiction with the contemporaneous record**: the pamphlet has Robert Morriss running the
   Washington Hotel in 1820, but "contemporary records show he did not start in that position until at
   least 1823" (Poundstone 1993) — a specific, checkable factual error in the narrative's own claimed
   timeline.
5. **Genre/literary-convention pattern**: the story's structure echoes known treasure-cipher *fiction*
   tropes predating or contemporary with it (Poe's "The Gold-Bug," 1843; Stevenson's *Treasure Island*,
   1883) — a genre-convention signature, not proof by itself but a named pattern worth tracking.

## Applied comparison against Oak Island's own record

| Beale signature | Oak Island equivalent, already on file |
|---|---|
| Anachronistic vocabulary in the "original" text | Not yet checked — the stone's inscription itself was never legible/transcribed reliably enough for a vocabulary-dating test (per this project's own extensive, disclosed uncertainty about the actual inscription content) |
| Traceable single-author origin for the "decoding" | **Partial match, weaker form**: not a single traceable author, but an *untraceable* anonymous attribution chain (unnamed schoolteacher → unnamed "Irish instructor") with a specific, verifiable misattribution (the real professor was Swiss, not Irish, and doesn't appear in the originating 1949 account) — a different but comparably suspicious pattern: fabricated/garbled provenance rather than a hidden single author |
| Structural implausibility | Not yet checked |
| Contradiction with the contemporaneous record | **Strong match**: the now-standard decoded wording ("Forty feet below...") has *no* 19th-century documentary basis at all — the 1894 source gives entirely different wording ("Ten feet below..."), meaning the "decoding" itself demonstrably changed between its two earliest documented appearances, 55 years apart, before settling into today's commonly-cited form in 1949 |
| Genre/literary-convention echo | Not yet checked — Oak Island's own Captain Kidd framing is itself a well-known 19th-century treasure-legend trope, worth a dedicated check against when Kidd-legend conventions specifically entered American treasure folklore |

## Honest assessment

**Two of five Beale-derived signatures show a real match or partial match in Oak Island's own
already-documented record** (shifting/undocumented decoded wording; an anonymous, internally inconsistent
attribution chain) — not proof of fabrication by itself, but a real, externally-calibrated data point
rather than an intuition call. **Three signatures are simply not yet checked** (vocabulary-dating is
blocked by the inscription's own illegibility; structural implausibility and genre-convention timing have
not been investigated). This is genuine, partial progress on SQ-4's stated deliverable (a signature
checklist applied to Oak Island's own claim), not a completed benchmark.

## Honesty precommitment, kept

This comparison does not conclude the Oak Island stone or cipher story is fabricated — it reports which
specific, externally-sourced signatures match, which don't apply yet, and which remain unchecked, exactly
as found.

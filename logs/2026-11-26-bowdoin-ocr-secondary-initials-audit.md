# Bowdoin OCR versus secondary L/N-initials claim

**Date:** 2026-11-26 08:56 ET  
**Scope:** primary-issue OCR reading and comparison with one modern secondary account; no visual page-image verification.

## Primary issue
Internet Archive identifies *Collier's: The National Weekly*, 1911-08-19, Vol. 47, Issue 22:
https://archive.org/details/sim_colliers-the-national-weekly_1911-08-19_47_22/sim_colliers-the-national-weekly_1911-08-19_47_22

Full-text OCR:
https://archive.org/stream/sim_colliers-the-national-weekly_1911-08-19_47_22/sim_colliers-the-national-weekly_1911-08-19_47_22_djvu.txt

On printed p. 20, the OCR says Bowdoin examined the Money Pit stone at Creighton's bookbindery in Halifax and describes it as “of a basalt type, hard and fine-grained” (OCR lines 7421–7436). In the sixth conclusion, it says there were no characters on the rock and that a few scratches were made by Creighton's employees (OCR lines 7823–7833; the OCR is visibly garbled after “but”).

## Secondary discrepancy
A modern research page says the only remaining carving was L and N initials cut by an unknown hand:
https://thecurseofoakisland.com/hunt/captain-henry-bowdoin-and-the-old-gold-salvage-company

That L/N detail is not present in the OCR passage of Bowdoin's Collier's article. It may derive from another witness, but that provenance has not been established here. Do not attribute it to the 1911 article without visual or textual confirmation.

## Limits and decision
This is an OCR-level reading of a digitized primary issue, not visual verification of the original pp. 19–20 images. No claim is made that the OCR is exact, that the stone never carried any mark, or that its present location is known. No paid scan, archive contact or cryptanalysis.

Keep “L/N initials” unconfirmed. Compare the original public page images with the OCR if they become accessible.

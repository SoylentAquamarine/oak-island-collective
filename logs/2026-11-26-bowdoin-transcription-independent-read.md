# Independent transcription read: Bowdoin's 1911 Collier's article

**Date:** 2026-11-26 01:55 ET  
**Status:** Independent reading of a modern transcription; NOT an authenticated original issue or page image.

## Source read
Jason Colavito's full web transcription of H. L. Bowdoin, “Solving the Mystery of Oak Island,” attributed to *Collier's*, 19 August 1911, pp. 19–20: https://www.jasoncolavito.com/solving-oak-island.html (article text at page lines 697–795).

A second modern transcription is in the M. R. Chappell September 1973 manuscript PDF: https://www.oakislandmystery.com/images/Les-MacPhie-Files/table-d/M%20R%20Chappell%20Manuscript%20Sept%201973.pdf.

## Reproduced from the Colavito transcription
- Bowdoin says he examined the stone while in Halifax in 1909, at Creighton's bookbindery.
- He describes the rock as “of a basalt type, hard and fine-grained.”
- His closing conclusions deny a treasure, a Smith's Cove tunnel, a beach ring-bolt, valuable boring recoveries and a system of characters on the stone.
- He allows that there were scratches made by Creighton's employees; this is not the same as an authenticated inscription.

## Discrepancy and limits
The Colavito page attributes the article to 19-Aug-1911. The Chappell manuscript transcription refers to an 11-Aug-1911 copy. This date discrepancy is unresolved without the original issue. Neither transcription is a facsimile, and neither establishes the stone's present location.

## Research decision
This reading is enough to reject the public claim that the stone was already lost in the 1860s as an established fact: the transcription reports a 1909 bookbindery sighting. The defensible public wording is “present location unverified,” while the 1909 sighting remains transcription-tier evidence pending original-page authentication.

No archive contact, paid scan, image access or cryptanalysis was performed. The 23-Feb-1912 Amherst Daily News issue and Blair reply remain unverified.

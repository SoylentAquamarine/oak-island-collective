## Draft request text

> Subject: Research inquiry — 1864 Hunter-Duvar / George Cooke correspondence re: Oak Island
>
> To Nova Scotia Archives reference staff,
>
> I'm researching the early documented history of the "Oak Island Money Pit" and a specific piece of
> correspondence from January 1864. Secondary sources describe a letter from John Hunter-Duvar (described
> as secretary of a "Historical Society of Nova Scotia" active in 1864 — not the modern Royal Nova Scotia
> Historical Society, which was founded in 1878) to George Cooke, secretary of the Oak Island Association,
> dated 2 January 1864, and Cooke's reply dated 27 January 1864, discussing an inscribed stone reportedly
> found in the Money Pit excavation.
>
> Do you hold, or know of, any manuscript collection, fonds, or finding aid that might contain this
> correspondence, or records of the 1864-era historical society Hunter-Duvar was associated with? I'd
> appreciate any pointer to where such records might be held, even if not at your institution.
>
> Thank you for your help.

## Why this specific framing

Names the exact correspondence, gives both correspondents' roles, and explicitly flags the institutional-
continuity question already resolved by this project (so the archivist doesn't have to untangle that
themselves) — precise enough that a reference archivist can search meaningfully rather than starting cold.

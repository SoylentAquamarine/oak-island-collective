# SQ-4 — assembling the consolidated signature checklist (the deliverable itself)

## Precommitment self-review (written before assembling)

**Known:** SQ-4's own stated scope names three deliverables: a source manifest, "**a signature
checklist derived from the comparator panel**," and an applied comparison against Oak Island's
own claim. Five cycles of real research exist — three comparator cases (Beale, Kensington
Runestone, Dare Stones, 15 named signatures total) plus two background checks (Kempton, Snow)
plus a direct cipher-key-inconsistency finding plus the spelling-test-infeasibility finding —
each already individually compared against Oak Island's record inside its own log file.

**Not done yet:** the actual consolidated checklist artifact itself has never been assembled.
The findings exist scattered across six separate log files; nobody has built the single
cross-case document SQ-4's own scope promises as a deliverable.

**Design and why it's non-circular:** this is pure synthesis, not new research — every verdict
below is copied from, and cites, an already-committed source log. No individual signature's
match/partial-match/no-match/foreclosed/unresolved status is re-litigated or upgraded during
consolidation; where a source log's own wording is a careful hedge ("partial, weaker match"),
that hedge is preserved exactly, not simplified into a cleaner-looking binary.

**Honesty precommitment:** if assembling the cross-case view surfaces a genuine new pattern, name
it explicitly as newly visible only once compiled together — but do not manufacture a dramatic
finding; a cross-case tabulation that mostly confirms what was already known in scattered form
is still the real, promised deliverable.

## The consolidated checklist

`data/sq4-fabrication-signature-checklist.md`, created this cycle, holds the full table. Summary
of the pattern visible once assembled:

**Recurring real or partial matches, across multiple independent cases**: Oak Island's record
matches, or partially matches, a convenient-promotional-timing signature in **both** Kensington
(books/Viking-revival audience) and its own case (the 1894 prospectus, the 1949 Snow
popularization); a commercial-monetization signature in Kensington; an anonymous/untraceable-
or-misattributed insider-chain signature in **three separate framings** (Beale's "traceable
single author," Kensington's "traceable insider-circle connection," Dare Stones' "names absent
from the record") — each independently converging on the same underlying fact already on file:
the 1949 attribution chain is anonymous and contains a specific, verifiable misidentification
(the real professor was Swiss, not Irish).

**Recurring structural foreclosures, not misses**: a physical-weathering test (Kensington) and a
linguistic-anachronism/vocabulary-dating test (Beale, Dare Stones) are each independently
foreclosed by the same single underlying fact — the original stone is reported lost, so no
physical or textual examination of its actual inscription is possible. This is one fact
producing multiple "not checkable" rows across different comparators, not three separate gaps.

**A genuinely informative disanalogy**: Dare Stones' collapse came from a direct, first-person
confession-level artifact (a named, living forger's own extortion attempt). Oak Island has no
equivalent — its evidentiary problems are all structural/provenance-based, never a confession.
This is a real difference in kind, not just degree, and is one this project's own record should
not treat as "less suspicious," per the Dare Stones log's own framing — a story can be
genuinely evidentially empty without anyone ever confessing to inventing it.

**What remains unchecked across every case**: a direct background check on the two anonymous
1949-era tellers' own history (beyond Kempton and Snow specifically, who are the two *named*
individuals in that chain) remains open — the signature itself ("a paid supplier/source with
documented prior forgery history") was named by Dare Stones specifically and is not resolved by
the Kempton/Snow checks, which addressed named individuals, not the anonymous schoolteacher/
"Irish instructor" layer of the chain itself, which remains unidentified and so cannot be
background-checked at all. This is a *different*, deeper gap than it first appears — not simply
"two checks done, more could follow," but a structural limit: the chain's most proximate layer
has no name to check.

## Outcome

The deliverable now exists as a real artifact, not just implied by scattered logs. The cross-
case view surfaces one genuinely new-to-state observation (the three-fold convergence on the
same anonymous-chain fact, and the structural-limit framing of the still-open background-check
gap) without inventing a new verdict on any individual signature.

## Decision

This closes out SQ-4's own named deliverable gap. The panel itself (comparator cases) is not
necessarily complete — a fourth comparator remains a standing option per the Dare Stones log's
own framing — but the specific, concrete, long-pending "build the actual checklist" task is
done.

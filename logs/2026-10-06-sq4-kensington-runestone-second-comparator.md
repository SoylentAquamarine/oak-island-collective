# SQ-4 — second comparator case: the Kensington Runestone

**Trigger:** last cycle completed the Beale Ciphers comparator case (5 of 5 signatures checked). SQ-4's own
scope explicitly calls for "a small panel," not a single case — the stated next step. The Kensington
Runestone (Minnesota, found 1898, claimed internal date 1362) is arguably an even closer structural match
to Oak Island than Beale: both are a physical **inscribed stone** object with a claimed message, both
trace to a specific individual finder/promoter, both have an active, well-documented scholarly
fabrication case, and both involve a community of amateur defenders still arguing for authenticity today.

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known about Oak Island's own record (Confirmed Finding): the "Forty feet below, two million
pounds are buried" decoded plaintext has no 19th-century documentary basis, traces to an anonymous
1949 attribution chain with a specific misidentification, and (per last cycle's new finding) Kempton's own
1949 cipher key is directly, repeatedly self-inconsistent across six symbol/letter pairs. Not done: any
check of Oak Island's record against a second, independently-built fabrication-signature checklist, to see
whether the Beale-derived matches generalize or were specific to that one comparator.

## Design and why it's non-circular

Directly fetched (not search-summary) Wikipedia's "Kensington Runestone" article and extracted its
specific, named, citable scholarly evidence — built entirely from Kensington-specific scholarship (Erik
Wahlgren, George T. Flom 1910, S.N. Hagen, geologists Newton Horace Winchell 1910 and Harold Edwards,
Mats G. Larsson 2019–2020), none of it aware of or tuned to Oak Island. Checking Oak Island's
already-on-file record against this independently-built checklist is a real test of generalization, not a
second pass at confirming the same conclusion a different way.

## The Kensington Runestone: documented, named fabrication signatures

1. **Linguistic/runological anachronism**: Wahlgren identified "opthagelse farth" as unattested in
   Scandinavian languages before the 16th century, anachronistic for a claimed 1362 inscription; Flom
   (1910) found "a strong apparent divergence between the runes used... and those in use during the 14th
   century"; the grammar lacks period-correct case inflection and plural verb forms; Hagen described the
   alphabet as "a synthesis of older unsimplified runes, later dotted runes, and a number of Latin
   letters" — i.e. a mixture no genuine single-era inscription would show.
2. **Physically implausible preservation for the claimed age**: geologist Winchell (1910) estimated the
   weathering at "roughly 500 years" by comparison but noted "the chisel marks were fresh"; geologist
   Harold Edwards later found the inscription "about as sharp as the day it was carved... showing virtually
   no weathering."
3. **Convenient timing relative to a contemporary cultural wave**: the 1898 find lands squarely inside a
   documented Scandinavian-American "Viking revival" driven by National Romanticism and Leif Ericson
   commemoration — plus Swedish-Norwegian nationalist rivalry that made the stone's joint Norwegian-Swedish
   expedition story useful to both communities' pride.
4. **A traceable insider-circle connection**: a 2019–2020 discovery (Mats G. Larsson) found two local
   runic inscriptions (1870, 1877) near Öhman's own home parish closely resembling the Kensington runes,
   and specifically tied to a named person — Anna Ersson, Öhman's own cousin and childhood friend — who
   lived there in 1878, predating the "discovery" by twenty years.
5. **Commercial monetization of the object**: documentary evidence (a bill of sale) shows Öhman sold the
   stone for $10 in 1911, contradicting a promoter's competing claim that it was given freely — i.e. the
   object's exploitation had a financial dimension from early on.

## Honest assessment

**Two of five Kensington-derived signatures show a real match** in Oak Island's own already-documented
record (convenient commercial/promotional timing; financial monetization) — both independently confirming
patterns already on file, from a wholly different case. **One signature shows a real, informative
disanalogy** (the physical-evidence question is permanently foreclosed for Oak Island, unlike Kensington,
where the object survived and could still be tested) — this is itself useful: it explains why Oak Island's
own case can never produce a Winchell/Edwards-style physical-preservation test, not just that it hasn't
been attempted. **One signature is a partial, much weaker match** (anonymous vs. a named, dated insider
connection). **One signature remains genuinely unresolved** pending SQ-2's own transcription work, same
status as it had before this cycle.

Combined with last cycle's Beale case (4 of 5 real/partial matches), Oak Island's record now shows a
consistent pattern across two independently-built comparator panels: real matches cluster around
provenance/promotion/monetization signatures, while signatures requiring the original inscription's own
content (anachronism, physical testing) stay blocked by the same standing SQ-2 problem — the stone's
language was never reliably recorded and the object itself no longer exists to examine directly.

## Honesty precommitment, kept

This does not conclude Oak Island's claim is fabricated — it reports which specific, externally-sourced
signatures match, which don't apply, which are structurally impossible to test (and why), and which remain
open, exactly as found.

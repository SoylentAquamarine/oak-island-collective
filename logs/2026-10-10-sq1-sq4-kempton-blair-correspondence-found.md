# SQ-1/SQ-4 — a contemporary 1949 rebuttal found: Blair told Kempton directly "considerable of it is far from the facts"

**Trigger:** following up on last cycle's own named lead from the Dare Stones comparator — a background
check on Kempton and the 1949-era tellers, matching the investigative-journalism approach (Sparkes) that
exposed that hoax. Searched for biographical/documentary material on Rev. A.T. Kempton specifically.

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known: the 1949 attribution chain (an anonymous schoolteacher → an anonymous "Irish instructor,"
the real professor later misidentified) is anonymous and contains a specific, verifiable error; the
decoded plaintext has no 19th-century documentary basis; Kempton's own 1949 cipher key is internally
self-inconsistent (six symbol/letter conflicts, already found). Not done: any check on Kempton's own
documentary record — correspondence, contemporaries' reactions, or anyone else's real-time response to his
claims in 1949.

## Design and why it's non-circular

Searched for Kempton biographically (not the cipher claim itself, which is already well-covered), then
directly fetched the most substantive-looking result — an independent researcher's (Richard Joltes,
`criticalenquiry.org`) page specifically analyzing Kempton's role, built on his own stated original archival
research (Nova Scotia Archives, R.V. Harris Papers) with specific box/item citations. Checked the source's
own credibility (self-published, 2006, no institutional affiliation, but with specific checkable archival
citations) before relying on it, rather than assuming authority either way.

## Honesty precommitment

Report Joltes's own analysis and his quoted primary-document excerpts as what they are — a directly-fetched
secondary analysis quoting named archival items, not this project's own direct view of the actual Nova
Scotia Archives documents, which remain unverified by this project itself.

## Result

**Kempton is an intermediary, not the inventor, by his own admission.** Per Joltes's analysis, quoting
Kempton's own words from a typescript (Nova Scotia Archives, R.V. Harris Papers, MG 1 Vol 384): Kempton
says he paid an **unnamed schoolteacher** for a manuscript account of Oak Island, and states plainly: *"The
teacher who wrote my MSS. did not give me any proofs of his statements."* The symbols themselves trace to
this same unnamed teacher, who (per Kempton's own account) attributed the decoding to "a very bright Irish
Teacher" — Joltes notes specific named candidates sometimes floated for this figure (James DeMille, James
Leitchi of Dalhousie) are **unsupported speculation**, since Kempton himself never names anyone.

**A genuinely new finding for this project: a documented, contemporary 1949 rebuttal.** Frederick Blair —
the actual person excavating Oak Island at the time — received Kempton's claims directly and replied in
writing (per Joltes, citing item 2364e) that **"considerable of it is far from the facts."** This is the
first contemporary, documented pushback this project has found from someone with direct first-hand
knowledge of the site, responding to Kempton's claims in real time rather than this project inferring
unreliability from provenance gaps alone.

**Further negative evidence, extending an existing finding**: Joltes reports no inscription appears in an
1896 syndicate prospectus or a 1936 *Popular Science* article — two more specific, dated sources predating
1949 that discuss Oak Island without any mention of a cipher, strengthening (not duplicating) this
project's existing "no 19th-century documentary basis" finding with two additional negative data points.

**A specific, checkable archival citation set, useful for a future targeted request**: Nova Scotia
Archives, R.V. Harris Papers, MG 1 Vol 384 — Kempton's letter to Blair (19 April 1949, item 2364b), Blair's
reply (item 2364e, dated either 22 or 29 April 1949 — a minor date discrepancy within Joltes's own page,
disclosed rather than resolved by guessing), and Kempton's typescript (items 2364f-h). This is a different,
more specific lead than the already-drafted 1864 Hunter-Duvar/Cooke request — not acted on or drafted into
a new request this cycle, consistent with this project's standing rule that contacting an institution needs
the user's own go-ahead.

**Disclosed tier**: Joltes's site is a self-published independent research project (no institutional
affiliation), but with specific, checkable archival item citations — the same evidentiary tier this
project has already relied on for other independent researchers (e.g. Doug Crowell, Les MacPhie's
compilations). Not this project's own direct view of the primary Blair/Kempton correspondence.

## Decision

Promoting to `knowledge-base/state.md`: Blair's documented 1949 rebuttal is a real, citable, new piece of
evidence strengthening the existing "no credible 19th-century basis" finding with a contemporary expert's
own contemporaneous skepticism, not just this project's own later provenance analysis. The exact archival
item numbers are recorded as a concrete lead for a future, more targeted user-authorized archives request.

# Bowdoin's 1911 Collier's article — transcription audit (cycle #126)

**Date:** 2026-11-20  
**Scope:** Text-only source audit. No image or archive scan was downloaded.

## Source inspected
Jason Colavito hosts a complete transcription of H. L. Bowdoin, “Solving the Mystery of Oak Island,” cited as *Collier's*, 19 August 1911, pp. 19–20:

https://www.jasoncolavito.com/solving-oak-island.html

The page's transcription was read in full (the Bowdoin section, including its opening historical narrative, account of the 1909 expedition, examination of the stone, and six conclusions). It states that Bowdoin examined the stone at Creighton's Halifax bookbindery, described it as hard, fine-grained basalt, and concluded that there had never been a system of characters carved on it. It also repeats the earlier reported reading “Ten feet below two million pounds lie buried.”

## Evidentiary tier and limits
This is a **hosted transcription**, not the original *Collier's* facsimile. The host identifies the original issue/date/page range, but this audit did not inspect the original magazine pages. Archive.org's issue record for 19 August 1911, volume 47, number 22, exists, but direct scan access was unavailable in this session.

A separate transcription in the Chappell manuscript gives 11 August 1911. That date discrepancy remains unresolved; do not silently treat the two dates as interchangeable. The exact original issue and page images must be checked before promoting any wording to a primary-facsimile reading.

## Research significance
The available transcription makes the content of Bowdoin's account directly reviewable and clarifies the claim relevant to the lost stone: Bowdoin says he saw the stone in Halifax and did not find a system of characters on it. This is evidence about what the 1911 article's transcription says, not proof that Bowdoin's account is accurate or that the original stone never carried an inscription.

The 23 February 1912 Frederick Blair reply remains a separate, unverified issue-level lead. UNB's title catalogue covers 1895–1914, but catalogue coverage does not establish that this individual issue survives.
